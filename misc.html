Side quests

Three things I like, and would happily pour my time into, are physics and simulation, human history and society, and video games.

My long-term daydream is where the three intersect: physics modelling of the “dynamics of history and sociology”, in the form of video games. Some more detailed thoughts →

Another possible combination is a “how we science” game — a time-management and role-playing game about how one gradually becomes a research scholar. It’s meant to stir up the excited, frustrated, warm, nostalgic feelings we scientists know, but it could do more: show the public how modern science is actually made. Not the Hollywood-type mad-scientist Dr. Villain who hand-builds bombs or gene weapons, but something traceable, falsifiable, testable — with an intrinsic logic that proceeds step by step. It could also surface the challenges science faces today: funding cuts, freedom and collaboration strained by politics, intrinsic bottlenecks in some disciplines, talent drawn away to industry. It isn’t meant to lecture, only to show — but showing itself is meaningful.

If you come across a game developed by “Jobless PhD Shelter game studio”, that’s me.
