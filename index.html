Zhichao “Carton” Zeng 曾志超

I am a theoretical/computational astrophysicist studying the nature of dark matter. I work primarily with N-body simulations, and I use them to bridge dark matter theory and observations, with a recent focus on small scales (galactic and sub-galactic).

My recent work primarily concerns self-interacting dark matter (SIDM): how gravothermal evolution and host–subhalo interactions drive the evolution of (sub)halos toward core formation or core collapse, and what imprints they leave on the systems we can actually observe. See the details of my SIDM ecosystem with idealized simulations here →

I am currently a postdoctoral researcher at Texas A&M University, working with Prof. Louis Strigari. Before this, I earned my Ph.D. in physics at The Ohio State University, advised by Prof. Annika H. G. Peter, with a thesis on the evolution of small subhalos/satellites in SIDM cosmology. Before that, I did my B.Sc. and M.Phil. at The Chinese University of Hong Kong with Prof. Ming-Chung Chu, working on cosmological neutrinos and large-scale structure. I was born in Xi’an, an ancient capital city in the northwest of mainland China, and moved to Hong Kong for college.
